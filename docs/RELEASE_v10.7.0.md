**Release title:** Shree Hari Antardhan, Adhika Hari Jayanti, and Refined Kshaya Host-Day Observance  

**Version:** 10.7.0  
**Type:** Minor release  
**Catalog:** 336 festival identities · 124 vrat identities

## Summary

Version **10.7.0** presents a fuller Satsangi calendar view: monthly Shri Hari Jayanti across the lunar year—including intercalary (Adhika) months—**Shree Hari Antardhan (Swadhaam Gaman)** on Jyeshtha Shukla Dashami with clear historical context and multilingual labels, and careful day placement when a lunar tithi does not appear at sunrise.

---

## Highlights

### Monthly Hari Jayanti in intercalary months

**Hari Jayanti** is the monthly remembrance of Bhagwan Swaminarayan on **Shukla Navami**, distinct from the annual Chaitra Hari-Nom celebration.

In **10.7.0**, that monthly Navami observance is published for ordinary months and for **Adhika (intercalary) months**, so the regular monthly remembrance remains visible when the lunar year carries an extra month, while the annual Chaitra day stays its own observance.

### Shree Hari Antardhan (Swadhaam Gaman)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Shree Hari Antardhan |
| **Also known as** | Hari Antardhan, Swadhaam Gaman, Antardhan Leela, Hari Tirodhan |
| **Lunar day** | Jyeshtha Shukla Dashami (Sud 10) |
| **Nature** | Commemorative remembrance day — **not** a mandatory fast |
| **Historical anchor** | Vikram Samvat 1886 Jyeshtha Sud 10 · **1 June 1830** · **Gadhada** |

Tradition observes this as Antardhan Tithi—remembrance, katha, dhun, and temple programmes—rather than a compulsory vrata. In the catalog it is a **festival** identity, not a fasting vrat.

When an Adhika Jyeshtha is present, this annual day follows nija-month placement, so it stays distinct from other Jyeshtha Sud 10 observances (for example Ganga Dussehra on the Adhika occurrence).

### Placement when a lunar day is missing at sunrise

Some years a target tithi never sits at either civil sunrise (**kshaya**). For selected observances, **10.7.0** places the day on the **civil host day** of that skipped tithi—the day that actually contains the lunar interval—when a “first sunrise day” reading does not apply.

When the same tithi appears at two consecutive sunrises (**vriddhi**), the first (purva) sunrise day remains preferred where that guidance applies.
